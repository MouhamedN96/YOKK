# Here are 15 examples of Wolof-English code-switching, commonly used by bilingual speakers in Senegal and The Gambia:

Dafa ma disturb ba ma stress.
(He/It disturbed me until I got stressed.)
Actually, li ma waxoon, dëgg la.
(Actually, what I said was true.)
Balla ma dem, dama koy confirm ba pare.
(Before I go, I will confirm it first.)
Mën na nekk, you know.
(It can be, you know.)
Lan moy sa nimero telefon?
(What is your telephone number?)
I promise I won't be late. Dina ñëw suba.
(I promise I won't be late. I will come tomorrow.)
Dama busy tey, mënuma ñëw.
(I am busy today, I can't come.)
Waññil price bi tuuti, su la neexee.
(Reduce the price a bit, please.)
Ndax mën nga ma help ci lii?
(Can you help me with this?)
Okay, ba beneen yoon.
(Okay, until next time.)
Dama la miss lool, sama xarit.
(I miss you very much, my friend.)
Wait for me, ma ngi ñëw.
(Wait for me, I am coming.)
Checkal lii, ndax baax na?
(Check this, is it good?)
Everything is fine, jamm rek.
(Everything is fine, peace only.)
Sorry, degguma li nga wax.
(Sorry, I didn't understand what you said.)
